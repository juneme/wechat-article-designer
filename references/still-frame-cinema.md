# Still-Frame Cinema

Use this route for a calm cinematic article built from full-width color fields, title cards, hard-edged aspect ratios, subtitle bands, split screens, film-strip swipes, and a closed credits ending.

## Evidence boundary

## Design fingerprint

- **Visual thesis**: pace the article as a sequence of still shots instead of an open editorial page.
- **Palette**: warm white, charcoal, mist blue, pale wine, and a small deep-wine transition accent.
- **Opening**: use a centered title card inside a full-width color field, bounded by a dark timecode bar and a short transition band.
- **Section rhythm**: alternate screens, subtitles, split shots, quiet intertitles, and hard cuts without rounded cards.
- **Image behavior**: use 16:9 and 21:9 environment frames, paired 3:4 close-ups, and a 16:9 film-strip swipe sequence.
- **Recurring motif**: scene labels, timecodes, HOLD/CUT language, subtitle alignment, sprocket bars, and credits.
- **Closing**: finish with a full-width end title and a closed dark credits block.

## Structural grammar

1. Keep the primary alignment centered and use full-width horizontal massing.
2. Use hard rectangles and stable aspect ratios with `min-height` fallbacks; avoid organic radii in this route.
3. Limit split-screen flex to two short panels whose text remains readable at 320px and stacks cleanly in fallback mode.
4. Use repeating linear gradients only for empty sprocket decoration with a solid fallback; do not encode content in the pattern.
5. Keep the film strip manual, ordered, and independently readable without scroll snap.
6. Preserve sufficient light reading fields between dark screens so the page remains comfortable rather than oppressive.

## Reuse boundary

Do not transfer the cinematic dark mass, title cards, timecodes, or hard aspect-ratio rhythm into an artbook-led brief by default. Use this route as a deliberate contrast family or borrow one dimension only.
