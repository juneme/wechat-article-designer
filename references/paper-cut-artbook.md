# Paper Cut Artbook

Use this route when a WeChat article needs a quiet contemporary art-book register, visible editorial decisions, and strong hierarchy without a dark hero, numbered chapters, or repeated rectangular cards.

The fragment passed Creative audit and 320px, 375px, and 390px source/fallback browser checks before operator validation. Editor version, device, pasted DOM, and property-by-property preservation are unknown.

## Design fingerprint

- **Visual thesis**: make whitespace, image silhouette, and copy density carry the design instead of a full-bleed color field.
- **Palette behavior**: keep about 70-80% of the article white or near-white. Use graphite for text and rules, two muted accent families for large secondary surfaces, and one warm correction color on less than roughly 5% of the page.
- **Opening mechanism**: use a small ruled masthead, one multi-line typographic statement, and an offset deck before the first image. Avoid a dark hero panel.
- **Section rhythm**: alternate open prose, one short colored field, and a deliberate pause. Do not place every section in a card.
- **Edge geometry**: assign different silhouettes by narrative role, such as an arch for the lead image, one asymmetric corner for a detail image, and a folded corner for a quote.
- **Image behavior**: let the lead image feel architectural and the supporting image feel like a clipped plate. Use a manual swipe strip as a contact sheet, not as a generic product carousel.
- **Recurring motif**: use editorial marginalia, plate captions, crop marks, shelf labels, or page-edge notes derived from the subject.
- **Closing device**: finish with an open ruled statement or small registration mark; do not repeat the opener as another large colored card.

## Structural grammar

1. Establish one full-column baseline and one inset baseline. Reuse them deliberately.
2. Let at least one chapter begin beside a short vertical or narrow marginal label, but keep the label readable if it falls back to horizontal text.
3. Create one explicit density contrast: a compact field followed by an open sentence with generous vertical space.
4. Use no more than three distinct image silhouettes in one article. Tie each silhouette to a stable image role.
5. Keep the swipe strip visually quieter than the lead image and expose part of the next item as the affordance.
6. Build folded corners with zero-size border triangles only when the triangle contains no semantic content.

## Palette recipe

The validated example used `#ffffff` and `#1c201f` as the reading field, muted lavender `#c7b9e8`, sage `#a8cbbd`, and small coral `#e97862`. Reuse the role relationship, not these exact values, unless the source material supports them.

Reject palettes where four or more saturated accents compete across large surfaces. Preserve near-black body text even when accent surfaces are pale.

## Compatibility contract

- Keep all essential structure in ordinary flow using `section`, `p`, and `span` with inline styles.
- Limit `clip-path` to a thin empty decoration; removal must produce a harmless rectangle.
- Limit `writing-mode` to a short label that also fits horizontally.
- Use `box-decoration-break` only on readable solid-background highlights.
- Pair a manual `overflow-x:auto` strip with ordinary source order and a static swipe cue; removing scroll snap must not block scrolling.
- Give every image placeholder a stable `min-height`; do not rely on a clipped shape to preserve its existence.

## Reuse boundary

Do not present a recolored Paper Cut sequence as a new exploration. When deriving another direction from it, change at least four of these seven dimensions: opener, section sequence, edge geometry, image silhouette, recurring motif, repeated-block treatment, and closing device.

Do not rely on that component count alone. `Contour Index / 07` changed the named components but still reproduced the same whole-page silhouette through a white field, large left-aligned title, oversized organic lead image, open editorial chapters, rounded secondary image, swipe strip, and airy ending. ## Artbook-led deep exploration

Prefer this route when the brief explicitly prioritizes color, artistry, beauty, harmony, tactile composition, or an editorial object-like reading experience. Deepen the artbook language instead of abandoning it after one successful page.

Vary the family through these independent axes:

1. **Color story**: keep one paper field, two related muted color families, and one correction color below roughly 4% of the page. Build harmony through shared temperature or shared gray content, not by making every hue equally pale.
2. **Paper construction**: suggest mounted sheets, folded inserts, belly bands, translucent tissue, cut windows, pasted labels, or registration strips with ordinary flow, solid fills, borders, alpha colors, and harmless border triangles. Do not depend on shadows or positioning.
3. **Spread logic**: compose chapters as cover, gatefold, insert, plate, margin note, contact sheet, colophon, or specimen envelope. Change the spread sequence rather than only changing corner radii.
4. **Image silhouette**: assign a small set of silhouettes to narrative roles. Explore arches, circles, capsules, scalloped impressions, clipped corner plates, tall bookmarks, and panoramic strips, but keep each image replaceable in the editor.
5. **Typographic collage**: combine one dominant statement with quiet captions, marginal labels, running heads, underlines, cloned highlights, or short vertical notes. Keep body copy stable at mobile reading sizes.
6. **Material rhythm**: alternate mounted color fields, open paper, image pauses, and compact annotations. Avoid both uninterrupted white scrolling and a page made entirely of colored cards.
7. **Ending object**: close with a colophon, envelope flap, index tab, bound edge, small plate, or deliberate paper fold rather than repeating the opener.

Useful subfamilies include botanical specimen book, museum conservation folio, cut-paper color atlas, fashion swatch archive, poetic zine, architectural material board, and seasonal field journal. Use them as semantic starting points and rebuild the whole-page massing for the article; do not merely rename the same Paper Cut sequence.

Before delivery, compare the new full-page thumbnail with prior artbook drafts. Remaining inside the artbook family is intentional, but at least four of the six gestalt axes must still change: first-screen mass, dominant alignment, container archetype, image-ratio rhythm, vertical density curve, and closing mass.

For a multi-variant request, compare every sibling against the others, not only against the original Paper Cut example. Define a different paper object, opener, chapter sequence, image-role set, density curve, and ending object before writing each variant. Shared artbook principles do not justify shared DOM skeletons; if two small full-page thumbnails read as the same layout with different colors or labels, rebuild one of them.
