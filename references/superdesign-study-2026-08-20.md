## Cross-source findings

- Strong systems separate field, ink, primary signal, secondary signal, and correction color. Accent colors are usually rationed by role.
- Typography is commonly a three-role relationship: display voice, reading voice, and optional mono/data voice. The relationship transfers to WeChat even when the web fonts do not.
- The most reusable composition knowledge is density change: declaration, evidence, pause, interpretation, and action do not receive equal visual weight.
- Hairlines, full-width bands, image turns, and type scale often provide more structure than card containers.
- Web motion usually encodes reading order. In WeChat it should become static layering, sequence, partial-frame cues, or explicit section progression.
- Bento grids, glass surfaces, sticky stages, drag interactions, and huge desktop type are common source patterns but require aggressive mobile translation.
- Only a minority of source prompts state evidence rules explicitly. The WeChat skill must add its own evidence boundary to every synthesis.
- The corpus contains both light and dark systems, serif/sans/mono relationships, hard and soft geometry, editorial and data-dense layouts, image-led and type-led openers, and restrained and saturated palettes. No one family is a default.
